Principles

How I think about shipping software.

— I.

Boring code is good code.

Surprises belong in birthday parties, not production. The cleverness budget goes into architecture, not the loop.

— II.

If you can't measure it, you didn't fix it.

Latency, throughput, error budget — write the number down before and after. Folklore is not a metric.

— III.

Services are a tax on coordination.

Pay it deliberately. A single well-bounded monolith beats four microservices owned by nobody.

Who I am
Cairo, 2025

A backend engineer who quietly keeps the lights on.

I'm Mohamed, a backend engineer based in Cairo with five years building production systems in C# and .NET for finance, government, and transportation. I care about the boring parts — clean APIs, sensible data models, honest validation — because that's what lets integrations between institutions run quietly.

Currently a Senior Software Engineer at iScore, the Egyptian Credit Bureau, I build the APIs and integration services that connect banks and the Digital Egypt Platform with national authorities — the Ministry of Interior, Public Prosecution, Administrative Control Authority, and GAFI. Before that, I spent four years at TransIT, building 6 production systems associated with the Egyptian Ministry of Transport — from real-time ride-hailing to hardware-integrated toll gates.
